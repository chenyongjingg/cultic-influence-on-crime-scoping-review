Dear Editors of the Research Topic *Understanding Cultic Influence on Crime: Psychological Manipulation, Group Dynamics, and Forensic Implications*,

We submit "Cultic influence on crime: a PRISMA-ScR scoping review of mechanisms, criminal outcomes and reporting practices" for consideration as a Systematic Review.

The manuscript sits where the Research Topic places the problem: at the junction of the psychology of influence and the criminal-justice process. Its contribution is not another narrative synthesis but a measurement of what this literature actually makes accessible to the reader who has to act on it.

We screened 628 records from six bibliographic sources against four pre-specified criteria; 163 met them, 142 of which carried an abstract. Twelve documented lexical detectors then measured what each abstract reports — group definition, participant role, involvement duration, standardised instrument, offence, legal outcome, jurisdiction — with every match returning its span, so any count in the paper can be re-derived from the deposited corpus.

The finding is a gap between what the field knows and what it shows. The variables that carry the causal claim are the least reported in the abstract: 7.0% state how the group was operationally identified, 15.5% distinguish participant roles, 1.2% name a standardised instrument. Yet a paired reading of abstracts against full texts — on the 42 records for which full text could be obtained — raises the standardised-instrument rate from 0% to 28.6% and the legal-outcome rate from 16.7% to 66.7%. The information is not missing from the studies; it is missing from the interface through which the literature is read. That is why the paper ends with a ten-item minimum reporting set, framed as a disclosure instrument a regulator could impose at low cost.

Two design choices may be of interest to the editors. First, the abstract/body/full-text comparison separates "not measured" from "not reported in the abstract", a distinction this literature has not previously quantified. Second, screening was machine-assisted, and rather than assert its quality we audited it: every decision was replayed from the stored fields, the 30 included records the audit could not confirm were read by the first author, and 28 were excluded before analysis. The audit, the worksheet and the verdicts are deposited with the paper.

The manuscript is original, is not published and is not under consideration elsewhere; all authors have approved it and declare no competing interests. The protocol is provided as Supplementary File 1, and all data and code are openly available at https://github.com/chenyongjingg/cultic-influence-on-crime-scoping-review.
